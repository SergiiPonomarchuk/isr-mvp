# How to Describe Your Community Values for ISR

## What You're Doing

You are about to describe **the kind of community future you want to create** and **the actions that should happen in it**.

This description will become a **vector (set of numbers)** that represents your vision of a good future community. These vectors will be combined with others' to create a **community values vector**. This community vector will answer one key question:

**"When our community members takes some actions, does it move us toward or away from the future we all said we want?"**

This is the single purpose: **Aligning community actions with community values.**

---

## What This Is NOT

- **Not about you**: We're not analyzing your personality or psychology
- **Not about judging you**: We don't judge whether you're "good" or "bad"
- **Not about predicting your behavior**: We can't know how you'll act
- **Not about your current life**: This isn't about what you do today, but what you believe *should* be

---

## What You Should Describe

Think about the community you want to see. Describe what should happen in it. Think about these 10 areas:

### 1. Freedom and Autonomous Choice

**In the community you want:**
- How should people be free to make their own choices?
- Should people be able to choose their own path, even if others disagree?
- Who decides what's right for someone—the person themselves, or others?
- What conditions must exist for people to truly choose freely?

**Examples of what to write:**
- "In the future community, people should be able to make their own medical decisions, even if doctors disagree"
- "I believe people should have the right to change their mind and choose a different path without punishment"

---

### 2. How We Care for Each Other

**In the community you want:**
- How should people relate to each other?
- Should relationships be deep and personal?
- Where does responsibility extend? (family only? strangers? everyone?)
- What does caring action look like in community?

**Examples of what to write:**
- "People should know each other deeply, not just as categories"
- "Community should include those unlike us, not just our friends"
- "We should care about distant people too, not just those near us"

---

### 3. Safety, Stability, and Basic Order

**In the community you want:**
- What makes it safe and predictable?
- How should community provide for basic needs (food, shelter, health)?
- What role do clear rules and structures play?
- How should risk be handled?

**Examples of what to write:**
- "Everyone should have access to shelter and food"
- "Community should have clear rules, but also allow them to change when needed"
- "Physical safety should be guaranteed"

---

### 4. Fair Treatment and Justice

**In the community you want:**
- How should resources be distributed?
- Should everyone get equal shares, or should effort matter?
- When someone has less than others, what should happen?
- When someone does serious wrong, what should community do?

**Examples of what to write:**
- "Everyone should start with equal opportunity, even if outcomes differ"
- "Basic needs should be met for everyone before anyone gets luxury"
- "When someone causes harm, the focus should be on restoring them to community, not just punishing"

---

### 5. Redemption, Restoration, and Second Chances

**In the community you want:**
- When someone has made serious mistakes, what happens?
- Should wrongdoers be exiled, or brought back?
- Can people truly change?
- What does forgiveness look like in action?

**Examples of what to write:**
- "People should be given second chances when they've done wrong"
- "The goal should be to restore people to community, not to punish forever"
- "Community should believe people can change and become different"

---

### 6. Authentic Expression and Living by Values

**In the community you want:**
- Should people be allowed to be fully themselves, even if different?
- Should people express their real beliefs, or conform to group norms?
- What happens when being authentic conflicts with group harmony?
- How important is it that people's actions match their stated values?

**Examples of what to write:**
- "People should be able to express unpopular views without being silenced"
- "Community should create space for people to be genuinely themselves"
- "When community says 'we believe in equality,' we should actually act that way"

---

### 7. Protecting the Vulnerable and Stewardship

**In the community you want:**
- Who needs special protection? (children? disabled people? sick people?)
- What does community owe to nature and environment?
- What about future generations—what do we owe them?
- Should cultural heritage be preserved, or can it change?

**Examples of what to write:**
- "The safety of children should be the first measure of whether something is good policy"
- "Community should care about environmental impact, even at some cost"
- "We should make decisions thinking about people not yet born"
- "Cultural practices that matter to communities should be honored and maintained"

---

### 8. Working Together and Sharing

**In the community you want:**
- How should important decisions be made? (by leader? by consensus? democratically?)
- Should resources be shared equally, or based on contribution?
- When should individual interest take second place to community good?
- Should important decisions be transparent and explained?

**Examples of what to write:**
- "Important decisions should be made together, not by one person"
- "Decisions should be explained so everyone understands why"
- "Shared resources should be managed fairly for everyone's benefit"
- "Sometimes people should put aside personal interest for the group"

---

### 9. Spiritual and Religious Practice

**In the community you want:**
- What role should spiritual or religious communities play?
- Should the community create space for spiritual practice?
- How should different spiritual traditions be honored?
- What does community owe to people's spiritual needs?

**Examples of what to write:**
- "People should be free to practice their spiritual/religious traditions"
- "Community should respect and provide space for shared spiritual practice"
- "Spiritual traditions that connect people to ancestors and future should be honored"

---

### 10. Digital Rights and Technology

**In the community you want:**
- How should personal data and digital privacy be protected?
- Should technology serve human needs, or should people adapt to technology?
- Who should control digital systems—corporations or communities?
- Should technology be accessible to everyone, or is it okay to exclude some?

**Examples of what to write:**
- "People should control their own personal data; it shouldn't be extracted"
- "Technology should serve human flourishing, not replace human connection"
- "Everyone should have access to digital tools and internet, not just wealthy people"
- "Digital systems should be governed democratically, not by companies alone"

## How to Write Your Description

### Length

Aim for **800–1500 words**.

- Less than 400 words usually lacks enough depth
- More than 2500 is usually too much
- Sweet spot: around 1000 words

### Style

- Write as if talking to someone you respect
- Use simple language; you don't need special terms
- "I think...", "It seems right that...", "In the future community..." are perfect
- Write what you actually believe, not what sounds impressive

### Three Key Principles

#### 1. Be Concrete, Not Abstract

**Weak**: "Community should value freedom"
**Strong**: "In the future community, people should be able to choose their medical care without others forcing different treatment"

**Weak**: "Justice matters"
**Strong**: "When someone is wronged, the community should work to repair the damage and restore them to the community, not just punish them"

#### 2. Be Honest, Not "Correct"

The system only works if you describe what you really believe, not what you think you *should* believe.

- If you prioritize family over strangers, say so (don't pretend equal care)
- If you value stability over change, say so (don't pretend you love risk)
- If you think some people's needs matter more than others, say so
- If you're uncertain, say that too

#### 3. Name Real Conflicts and Choose

Real community values create real conflicts. When values clash, something has to give. Tell us which one wins:

**Examples of real conflicts:**
- Freedom vs. Safety: "When someone's freedom puts others at risk, I think safety takes priority"
- Truth vs. Kindness: "When truth would hurt someone, I think... [your choice]"
- Individual Need vs. Group Good: "When my needs conflict with the community's, I think..."
- Justice vs. Mercy: "When someone has done harm, I think community should focus on... [justice/restoration/balance]"
- Protecting Tradition vs. Allowing Change: "When culture changes in ways that upset some people, I think..."

**These conflict statements are some of the most important things you'll write.**

## Simple Structure You Can Follow

You don't need headings, but this might help organize your thinking:

### Section 1: Your Vision of a Good Community (1–2 paragraphs)

What would your future community look like? How do people relate? How do they decide? What matters most?

### Section 2: Your Core Beliefs About Each Domain (6–8 paragraphs)

Pick the 6–8 domains from above that matter most to you. For each:
- State what you believe should happen in community
- Give a concrete example or scenario
- Connect it to action: "This means community should..."

### Section 3: Where Your Values Conflict (3–4 paragraphs)

Name 2–4 real conflicts you feel:
- Describe the conflict
- Say which side matters more to you
- Explain briefly why
- Give an example if you can

### Section 4: Areas You're Still Thinking About (1–2 paragraphs, optional)

Where are you genuinely uncertain? Where do you see validity on both sides? What are you reconsidering?

## What Happens Next

Once you submit your description:

1. **Vectorization**: AI extracts your values and converts them into numbers (a 233-dimensional vector)

2. **Clarification**: If anything is unclear, you may get 1–3 clarifying questions to make sure we understand correctly

3. **Community Vector**: Your vector is combined with others in your community to create a **community vector**

4. **Action Assessment**: When your community considers an action, the system asks: "Does this move us toward the future our community described?"

5. **Results**: You may see feedback like:
   - "✓ This action aligns with your community values"
   - "✗ This conflicts with what your community stated"
   - "⚠ This creates tension between values A and B; your community weight is X"

---

## Important Reminders

**This describes your vision of the future, not who you are today**
- You don't have to be perfect or living these values now
- You're describing what *should* be, not what *is*

**Be honest about your actual beliefs**
- The system only works if you tell the truth
- There are no "good" or "bad" values we judge against

**This is for community alignment, not personal judgment**
- Nobody uses this to judge you as a person
- The purpose is: "Do our actions match what we said we believe?"

## Questions?

**Q: What if I'm unsure about something?**
A: Write that you're uncertain and why. That's valuable information.

**Q: What if my values contradict?**
A: That's normal. Name the contradiction and tell us which usually wins when you have to choose.

**Q: Should I describe what I do, or what I believe should be?**
A: Describe what you believe *should be*. Not what you currently do.

**Q: Will this be used to judge me?**
A: No. It's only used to assess whether community actions align with community values.

**Q: Can I change my description later?**
A: Yes, values evolve. You can update it anytime.
